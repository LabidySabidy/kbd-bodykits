## 1. Brand identity

**Company:** KBD Body Kits (parent: American Plastic Technologies)
**Founded:** "Almost 50 years" — use this exact phrasing rather than a specific year. Do not write "Est. 1981" or any other dated claim.
**Manufactured in:** Orange County, California (Fullerton, CA specifically). Both phrasings are acceptable. "Made in USA" is the marketing umbrella.
**Owner / decision-maker:** Irving (NY roots, CA-based)
**Phone:** (877) 399-3794
**Socials:** Instagram `@kbdbodykits`, Facebook `kbdbodykits`, TikTok `@kbdbodykits`

### Product/material language

- Always say **"polyurethane"** or **"PU"** (never "urethane" alone, never "plastic," never "fiberglass" except as a contrast).
- The warranty is the **"Limited Lifetime Warranty"** — the word "Limited" must always appear. Never say just "Lifetime Warranty" or "Lifetime Guarantee" as a standalone claim.
- Key product attributes (in order of marketing importance):
  1. **Virtually indestructible / won't crack or shatter** (PU flexes on impact and returns to shape)
  2. **OEM quality fitment** (injection-molded, test-fitted to actual vehicles before production)
  3. **Made in the USA** (Orange County, full in-house manufacturing)
  4. **Limited Lifetime Warranty**
  5. **Designed from customer demand** (community-driven product development)

### Brand voice

- Direct, technical, builder-to-builder. Not flowery.
- Treats the customer as someone who knows what they're doing.
- Pride in manufacturing, pride in American-made, pride in the community.
- Avoids generic e-commerce language ("our amazing products," "shop now and save").

### Strategic positioning (this redesign)

**Drift-forward but inclusive.** Drift is clearly Irving's dominant customer base and where he spends sponsorship dollars. The hero, KBD Equipped showcase, and primary imagery should lean drift. But Jeep Wrangler, Mustang, Bronco, and Corvette owners need equal-class browsing experiences. Don't make non-drift customers feel like second-class citizens — they're paying his bills too.

## 2. Visual language

### Color palette (final)

**Anchors:**
- `--red: #CC0000` (primary brand red — keep exactly this, matches existing KBD)
- `--white: #FFFFFF` (clean white for product/results pages)
- `--black: #0C0C0C` (near-black for hero sections, nav, footer)

**Supporting:**
- `--cream: #F5F2EC` (warm off-white — Kith/Carhartt WIP territory. Use for section backgrounds where pure white feels cold)
- `--concrete: #1A1A1A` (slightly lighter black for card surfaces on dark backgrounds)
- `--steel: #2A2825` (warm dark for body text)
- `--ash: #7A7570` (muted secondary text)
- `--fog: #E8E6E2` (warm light gray for borders, dividers)

**Accent (use sparingly):**
- `--rust: #8B2C1C` (deep oxidized red — for hover states, secondary CTAs, vintage-poster moments. Never as primary)

### Typography

- **Display:** Barlow Condensed (900 weight for headlines, italic for emphasis tokens) — keep this from v1, it's correct.
- **Body:** DM Sans (400/500/600) — keep this from v1, it's correct.
- **No additional fonts.** Two is the budget.

### Aesthetic mood (Kith / Carhartt WIP / motorsport heritage)

- Off-white > pure white. Pure white only on results/product pages where catalog clarity matters most.
- Slight texture in dark sections — subtle grain, asphalt-feel backgrounds. Not loud.
- Editorial photography over product-cutout photography. Show cars in environments (paddock, parking lot at dusk, garage, track) rather than floating on white.
- Vintage motorsport poster influence in numerical callouts (large condensed numbers, small caps labels).
- Generous whitespace. Don't crowd.
- Sharp corners or 6–8px radius max — no big rounded "consumer app" radii.

### Iconography

- All icons SVG. **No emoji anywhere in the UI.**
- Stroke weight 1.5–2px, never filled unless brand requires (cart, heart for wishlist).
- Social icons must be real brand glyphs (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube), not letter monograms.

**Direction:**
- Every image must be a tuner/drift/project car. Lower-budget, modified, lived-in. Not concours-quality showroom shots.

- Environments: parking lots, paddock, drift events, garage interiors, twilight street shots. Not white seamless backgrounds for hero imagery (those are fine for product detail catalog shots).

## 12. Technical constraints (this is the production target)

**Target stack:** Headless WordPress (existing data layer) + React/Next.js frontend. The mockup files should be HTML/React prototypes that demonstrate the visual + UX direction. They are not the production code. **They are clickable Figma.**

**This means:**
- All cart state can live in localStorage (no backend needed)
- All "Submit" actions can resolve to a fake success state
- All product/build data lives in inline JS constants
- The Equipped-print page can keep its auto-print behavior, BUT add a 2-second delay and a "Cancel print" button so a reviewer can read it before the dialog pops

**Filename convention (CRITICAL):** Files use underscores: `KBD_Homepage.html`, `KBD_Results.html`, `KBD_Product.html`, `KBD_Equipped.html`, `KBD_Equipped-print.html`, `KBD_Will_Make_It.html`, `KBD_Checkout.html`. All inter-page links MUST use underscores. v1 incorrectly used spaces in link strings (`KBD Homepage.html`), breaking 48 navigation links. This must be fixed in every page.

**Tweaks panel:** v1 homepage references `tweaks-panel.jsx` via `<script src="tweaks-panel.jsx">` which doesn't exist and throws a console error. Remove this line, and remove all `useTweaks` / `TweaksUI` / `TWEAK_DEFAULTS` references — they're scaffolding from a different tool that doesn't belong in the final deliverable.
